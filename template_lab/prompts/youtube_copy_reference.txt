Ready-to-paste description
A falling ball speeds up even though nothing is pushing it harder—so where does that extra motion come from?

In this IGCSE Physics lesson, you will learn how energy moves between stores, how energy is transferred, and how to calculate kinetic and gravitational potential energy.

By the end of this lesson, you will understand:

• The principle of conservation of energy
• Why energy is transferred rather than created or destroyed
• The main energy stores used in physics
• Kinetic energy
• Gravitational potential energy
• Chemical energy
• Elastic energy
• Nuclear energy
• Electrostatic energy
• Internal or thermal energy
• The four main energy-transfer pathways
• Mechanical work
• Electrical work
• Heating
• Energy transfer by waves
• How to interpret simple energy-flow diagrams
• How to calculate kinetic energy using Ek = ½mv²
• Why doubling speed quadruples kinetic energy
• How to calculate changes in gravitational potential energy using ΔEp = mgΔh
• Why friction does not destroy energy
• How to read Sankey diagrams
• How arrow width represents energy transfer
• How useful and wasted energy appear in real systems

Worked examples include:

1. Calculating the kinetic energy of a 2 kg object moving at 3 m/s
2. Comparing kinetic-energy gain with gravitational-potential-energy loss
3. Describing energy transfers in a kettle
4. Comparing GPE and KE when a motor lifts a moving load
5. Reading useful and wasted transfers from a Sankey diagram

🧠 Try this challenge:

A 4 kg object falls through a height of 5 m.

Take g = 10 N/kg.

1. How much gravitational potential energy does it lose?
2. If all of that energy becomes kinetic energy, what is its kinetic energy just before impact?
