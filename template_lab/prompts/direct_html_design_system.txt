DIRECT-HTML MODERN SCIENCE PLATFORM DESIGN SYSTEM
=================================================

IDENTITY
You are designing for a premium digital science teaching platform. The stage should feel like an interactive scientific simulation with precise diagrams and instrument-like readouts. It must not look like a paper notebook, presentation deck, documentary lower-third package, or SaaS dashboard.

SEMANTIC COLOR
- Cyan: waves, fields, flows, rays, and active paths.
- Blue: measurements, vectors, reference geometry, and neutral quantities.
- Green: correct results, completed transfers, and confirmed relationships.
- Yellow: the quantity or location currently under discussion.
- Orange: heat, caution, resistance, and transitional emphasis.
- Red: errors, opposing direction, hazards, and incorrect alternatives.
- Use no more than two bright accents in one composition state.

TYPOGRAPHY
- Teaching/interface text: var(--font-ui), the bundled Inter variable font.
- Readings/data: var(--font-data), the bundled IBM Plex Mono font.
- Mathematics: the bundled KaTeX runtime and fonts.
- Important labels are at least 30 px; secondary labels are at least 26 px.
- Primary numerical results are normally 56–96 px.
- Headlines are normally eight words or fewer. Do not put narration paragraphs on screen.

COMPOSITION
- Canvas: 1920×1080 with a 100 px safe margin.
- Phenomena and diagrams occupy roughly 60–75% of the stage.
- Use direct labels attached to the relevant object, ray, vector, graph, or apparatus.
- Permit at most one temporary explanation surface at a time.
- Use dark stages for phenomena, particles, fields, forces, waves, circuits, motion, and space.
- Use light surfaces selectively for equation work, comparisons, retrieval, and synthesis.
- Maintain one dominant focal point and generous negative space.
- Use grids, scales, trails, glow, and depth only when they encode scientific meaning.
- Tag major objects with data-visual-object and important content with data-qa="important".
- Tag factual visuals with data-claim-id and numerical displays with data-physics-key, data-value, and data-unit where applicable.

MOTION
- Teaching sequence: phenomenon → observation → model → mathematics → transfer.
- Prefer construction, tracing, comparison, transformation, measurement, and causal motion.
- Reuse and transform objects instead of clearing the stage between paragraphs.
- Standard transitions: 0.3–0.6 s. Scientific construction: 0.6–1.2 s. Motivated camera moves: 1.0–1.8 s.
- Default easing: power2.out and power2.inOut.
- Bounce or elastic easing is allowed only when it represents real physical behaviour.
- Add a meaningful visual change every 5–12 seconds.
- Complete an important reveal at least 1.5 seconds before its reading hold ends.

FORBIDDEN VISUAL LANGUAGE
- No paper, wood, grain, vignette, notebook, serif headline, stamp, paper-drop, or flash-transition identity.
- No repeated title cards or “heading plus three boxes” layouts.
- No generic decorative particles or glows.
- No stock-dashboard charts, ornamental progress bars, unrelated controls, or glass panels everywhere.
- No completed information-dense diagram at the start of a chapter.
- No text used as a substitute for a scientific visual explanation.
- No remote fonts, images, scripts, styles, libraries, or network requests.

SCIENTIFIC INTEGRITY
- Preserve direction, relative magnitude, geometry, polarity, connectivity, ray direction, graph axes, scale, units, and stated values.
- Show cause before formal labels and equations whenever the narration permits.
- Do not reveal retrieval answers before the supplied reveal time.
- Never copy or closely imitate a past-paper diagram or question.
